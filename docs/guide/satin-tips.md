# Satin tips

A **satin column** is stitches that cross from one edge to the other. It looks smooth and shiny. It is the stitch behind most borders and letters.

![Satin column, with density and width marked](diagrams/satin-vs-fill.svg)

## Pick a width

- **Under 1.5 mm:** too thin. The stitches get tiny and can pile up. Use a **Triple** run instead.
- **2 to 6 mm:** the sweet spot.
- **Over 6.8 mm:** stitches get long and can snag. Lilo splits the column in two stitched halves on its own, or you can use a fill.

## Density

**Density (spacing)** is the gap in millimetres between needle drops on the same side of the column. Smaller is denser.

- **0.40 mm** is the standard starting point for 40 wt thread.
- **0.35 mm** is about the tightest for 40 wt. Below that you risk thread breaks and holes.
- 60 wt thread needs a tighter gap, about 0.35 mm, because the thread is thinner.

The hint under the slider shows stitches per centimetre.

## Split above

**Split above** divides columns wider than this many millimetres into two halves, so no stitch is too long. Zero turns it off. Two controls appear when it is on:

- **Stagger cycles** repeats the pattern of the split line.
- **Stagger amount** shifts the split line sideways, so the join does not form a visible seam.

## Short stitches on curves

On the inside of a tight curve stitches bunch up. **Short stitches on curves** shortens some of them so the column stays even. Turn it on for curved borders and round letters.

## Underlay and pull compensation

Always use some underlay under a satin column wider than 1.5 mm. Read [Underlay and pull compensation](underlay-and-pull.md).

## Pick the stitch order

Sew a long satin column in one go, not as several short columns joined end to end. Lots of joins create thread jumps.

## Test first

Sew a 20 mm test strip at your chosen width and density on scrap cloth. Look for gaps (loosen less) and ridges or puckers (loosen more).
