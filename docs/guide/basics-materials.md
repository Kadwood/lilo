# Embroidery basics: stabiliser, hoop, thread and bobbin

An embroidery machine does not move the cloth like a normal sewing machine. It holds the cloth still in a frame and moves the frame under the needle. These four things make that work.

## Stabiliser

**Stabiliser** is a sheet of backing you put under the cloth. Think of it as the paper you draw on when you do not want a thin page to crumple. Thousands of stitches pull on cloth. Without a backing, the cloth bunches up around the design.

- **Cut-away** stays in the garment. You trim around the design. It is the safe choice for stretchy cloth and anything you wash a lot.
- **Tear-away** rips off after sewing. It suits stable, tightly woven cloth.
- **No-show mesh** is a soft cut-away for thin cloth like a shirt or a suit lining. It does not scratch and does not show through.
- **Water-soluble topping** sits on top of fluffy cloth like towel, so stitches do not sink into the pile.

The **Sewing setup** card tells you which type fits your fabric. Read [Sewing setup presets](sewing-presets.md).

## Hoop

The **hoop** is the frame that holds cloth and stabiliser flat and tight. It has two rings. You press the cloth between them and tighten a screw.

The machine can only sew inside the hoop. Every hoop has a **sewing area**, a little smaller than the frame itself. Lilo draws that area on the canvas and warns you if your design sticks out. Read [Sizing and hoops](sizing-and-hoops.md).

A good hoop job feels like a drum. The cloth is tight but not stretched. Stretched cloth springs back after sewing and puckers the design.

## Thread

The **top thread** is what you see. Most embroidery uses **40 wt** thread. The "wt" number says how thin it is. A bigger number means thinner thread. **60 wt** is finer. It lets you sew smaller, crisper details, like tiny letters.

Thread comes in **brands** and each brand has its own colour codes. Lilo knows many. Read [Thread brands and matching](thread-brands-matching.md).

## Bobbin

The **bobbin** is a small spool of thread under the needle plate. It makes the back of the stitch. Embroidery bobbin thread is usually thinner and often white or black.

A good sew-out shows only a little bobbin thread on the front. If you see a lot of bobbin thread, the top thread is too tight. Read [Fixing sew-out problems](sewout-troubleshooting.md).

## Needle

Use a fresh needle. A dull needle shreds thread and punches holes. Lilo's Sewing setup suggests a size for your fabric and thread, like **75/11** for most cloth or **70/10** for a thin lining.
