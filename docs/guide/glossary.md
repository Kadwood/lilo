# Glossary

Words are in alphabetical order. A word in **bold** inside a meaning has its own entry.

## A to C

- **Appliqué.** A patch of cloth stitched onto a garment. An **E-stitch** edge is often used to hold it.
- **Aspect ratio.** The relationship between width and height. Locking it keeps a shape from squashing.
- **Auto digitize.** Lilo turns a picture into stitches in one step. Read [Turn a picture into stitches](auto-digitize.md).
- **Backing.** Another word for **stabiliser**.
- **Bean stitch.** Another name for a **triple** run.
- **Birdnesting.** A tangle of thread under the cloth that jams the machine.
- **Bobbin.** The small spool under the needle plate that makes the back of the stitch.
- **Colour block.** A run of shapes sewn in one thread, with no thread change inside.
- **Colour change.** A stop to swap the top thread for the next colour.
- **Column.** A **satin** stripe. Its width is the distance across it.
- **Cut-away.** A **stabiliser** you trim around the design. It stays in the garment.

## D to H

- **Density.** How close stitches sit together. A smaller gap means higher density.
- **Digitizing.** Turning a picture or drawing into stitch instructions.
- **DST.** A file format used by many commercial machines. It does not keep thread colours.
- **E-stitch.** A comb-shaped run stitch.
- **Edge outline.** A running line sewn round the edge of a **fill**.
- **Fill.** Rows of stitches covering an area.
- **Font (embroidery).** Letters drawn to be sewn, with a height range where they sew well.
- **Format.** The kind of file a machine reads, like PES or DST.
- **Gradient.** A fade in a **fill**, made by changing row spacing across the shape.
- **Hoop.** The frame that holds cloth tight while it is sewn.

## I to O

- **Inset.** How far inside the edge an **underlay** stops, so it stays hidden.
- **Jump.** Thread that travels from one point to another without sewing.
- **Knit.** Stretchy cloth. It needs more **pull compensation** and a firm **stabiliser**.
- **Lilo Link.** The part of Lilo that sends designs to a machine over Wi-Fi.
- **Lock stitches.** Tiny stitches at the start and end of a run that stop it unravelling. Also called tie-in and tie-off.
- **Manual stitch.** A stitch placed exactly where you click.
- **Monogram.** Initials sewn as a mark, like "JK" inside a jacket.
- **Motif.** A small shape repeated across a **fill**.
- **Needle drop.** One point where the needle goes through the cloth. Also called a stitch.
- **Needle size.** The thickness of the needle, like 75/11.
- **Origin.** The point in the design that sits at the centre of the hoop.

## P to S

- **PES.** The file format of Brother and Baby Lock machines. It keeps thread colours.
- **Pile.** The fluffy surface of cloth like towel or velvet.
- **Pull compensation.** Making a shape slightly bigger to allow for the thread pulling cloth inward.
- **Pucker.** Ripples in the cloth around a design.
- **Realistic view.** A preview that draws each stitch as shaded thread.
- **Registration.** How well one shape lines up with its neighbour.
- **Row spacing.** The gap between rows of stitches in a **fill**.
- **Run.** A stitch that follows a line.
- **Satin.** Side-to-side stitches in a column. Smooth and shiny.
- **Sew-out.** The real stitched result.
- **Sewing area.** The part of the hoop the machine can sew.
- **Stabiliser.** A sheet under the cloth that stops it moving and bunching.
- **Stitch length.** The distance between two needle drops.
- **Split satin.** A wide **satin** column divided so no stitch is too long.

## T to Z

- **Tatami.** The common name for a standard **fill** with offset rows.
- **Tear-away.** A **stabiliser** that rips off after sewing.
- **Thread weight.** How thin thread is. 40 wt is standard. 60 wt is finer.
- **Tie-in and tie-off.** See **lock stitches**.
- **Tolerance.** How far a stitch may stray from the line you drew.
- **Topping.** A thin water-soluble sheet on top of fluffy cloth.
- **Trim.** A cut of the thread between shapes, done by the machine.
- **Underlay.** Light stitches sewn first, under the top stitches.
- **Underpath.** Travelling through a **fill** under the stitches instead of jumping.
- **Zig-zag underlay.** An **underlay** that goes back and forth across a column.
