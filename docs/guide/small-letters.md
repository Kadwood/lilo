# Small letters

Thread has thickness. Make letters small enough, and the thread fills the gaps inside the letters ("e" and "a" turn into blobs).

## The limits

| Thread | Smallest letter height |
| --- | --- |
| **40 wt** (standard) | **6 mm** |
| **60 wt** (fine) | **4 mm** |

**Height** here means the height of a capital letter.

Lilo checks this for every font. If your letters are shorter than the limit, a warning shows under the height box and in the warnings tray. It offers to switch to 60 wt thread.

## How to sew small text clean

1. Use **60 wt** thread. Change **Thread weight** in the Sewing setup. Then use a 65/9 or 70/10 needle.
2. Choose **Premium** quality. It spaces narrow columns a bit wider (0.45 mm) so they do not clog.
3. Pick a plain, sturdy font. Skip fonts with hairlines.
4. Use **cut-away** stabiliser. It gives the cleanest detail. Read [Embroidery basics](basics-materials.md).
5. Sew a test on scrap cloth first.

## Do not swap the thread without redigitizing

If you design for 40 wt and then put 60 wt thread on the machine, you lose coverage. Change **Thread weight** in the Sewing setup instead, so Lilo tightens the spacing.

## Letters on the inside of a jacket

A 5 mm "JK" on a shirt-weight lining: **Shirting / lining**, **60 wt**, **Premium**, a font that suits 5 mm. Expect the letters to shrink slightly in the wash and the pull of the thread.
